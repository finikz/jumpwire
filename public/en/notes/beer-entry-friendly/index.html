Wheat Beer, Witbier and Hazy IPA: What Makes a Beer Easy to Enjoy?

“Soft” is not a style. It can come from different things.

JUMPWIRESeptember 14, 20263 min read

Some beers arrive softly; others make an entrance. Wheat beer, witbier and hazy IPA are often described as approachable, but they do not taste the same, and none is guaranteed to be free of bitterness, acidity or body.

Wheat beer: grain and foam

Wheat malt can contribute a softer texture, foam and a hazy appearance. German wheat beers often show yeast notes such as banana and clove. Belgian witbier may use spices and follow a different flavour path. Ask which kind of wheat beer it is rather than simply ordering “a white one.”

Hazy IPA: hops are still centre stage

Hazy IPA is still part of the IPA family, often with prominent fruity hop aromas. Body and bitterness vary from beer to beer. The bitterness can feel softer, but that does not mean it is absent; haze is not proof of poor control or higher quality.

Turn “easy to drink” into a useful order

Try: “I’d like something fruity, not too bitter, and not too heavy. Do you have a wheat beer or hazy IPA?” You can add whether you prefer banana and clove, citrus or tropical fruit. Specific preferences are a better guide than a vague label.

Takeaway

“Easy to drink” is a personal review, not a style. Say what feels comfortable and it is easier to find your next beer.
