CRAFT BEER NOTES · 01

What Are We Drinking When We Drink Craft Beer?

Four ingredients, a cast of microbes and flavour coordinates you do not have to memorize.

JUMPWIRESeptember 14, 20263 min read

Imagine a glass of beer as a small band: water builds the stage, malt lays down the foundation, hops bring aroma and bitterness, and yeast turns sugar into alcohol, carbon dioxide and a range of flavours. What the band sounds like depends on how the brewer arranges it.

First, an important twist: craft beer is not one fixed flavour

It is not always more bitter or stronger, and colour or alcohol alone cannot tell you what it tastes like. The term “craft beer” is used differently across countries and markets. For drinkers, it is more useful to look at the specific beer, style, ingredients and brewery description.

Four ingredients, four different jobs

Water is the main ingredient. Minerals affect brewing and sensory balance, but “soft” or “hard” water cannot define a beer on its own.

Malt supplies fermentable sugars and can contribute grain, biscuit, caramel or roast notes. Dark does not automatically mean strong; pale does not mean flavourless.

Hops can add bitterness and aromas of citrus, flowers, herbs, pine or tropical fruit. Variety, timing and process matter—hops do not mean “only bitter.”

Yeast ferments the sugars and can influence fruit and spice aromas. Different yeasts and conditions create different results; yeast is more than a behind-the-scenes sugar converter.

Why can craft beers taste so different?

Ingredients are only the beginning. Malt proportions, hop varieties and timing, yeast, fermentation temperature, maturation and the brewer’s choices all take part. A style name is like a map legend: it points in a direction but cannot write the whole story of every beer.

Craft beer is not an entrance exam

You do not need to remember the vocabulary. Be specific about what you like: crisp or full-bodied, pronounced aroma or low bitterness, tartness or roast. Check the beer description and ABV when you order; each style contains many personalities.

Takeaway

You do not have to understand craft beer before you enjoy it. Curiosity is already a way in.
