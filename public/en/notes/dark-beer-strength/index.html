Is Dark Beer Stronger? Colour Can Fool You

The malt was roasted. That is all the colour promises.

JUMPWIRESeptember 14, 20263 min read

Malt can be kilned and roasted to different degrees, bringing grain, biscuit, caramel, coffee or cocoa notes and changing the beer’s colour. A small amount of dark malt can deepen a beer significantly; that does not mean the brewer also turned the alcohol all the way up.

Four things colour cannot tell you by itself

Check ABV (alcohol by volume) instead of guessing from a dark appearance. Bitterness relates mainly to hops and overall balance; roast malt can contribute roast flavours or a light bitterness, but that is not the same as hop bitterness. Body is the weight and texture on your palate, and colour alone cannot predict it.

A dark beer can be dry and light or rich and full-bodied. A pale beer can still have a high ABV. Your eyes can read colour, but they cannot measure alcohol.

Ask a better question when ordering

Ask for the ABV and then about the body: “How strong is this one? Is it dry or sweet and full? Is the roast noticeable?” If you want a dark beer without a heavy body, ask whether there is a dry, lower-alcohol option. Let the bar team recommend from the actual menu.

Takeaway

Dark beer does not mean “black plus strong alcohol.” Check the ABV, then let your palate decide.

The malt chapter of “The Song of Malt: A Brief History of Beer” notes how malt roasting affects colour without making colour a direct description of taste.
