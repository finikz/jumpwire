IPA, Wheat Beer and Fruit Beer: What Is the Difference?

Three styles, three flavour routes. Choose where you want to begin.

JUMPWIRESeptember 14, 20263 min read

A style can come in many versions. Ingredients, yeast, brewing methods and the brewery all shape the final flavour, so “usually has this aroma” does not mean every beer must taste the same. Check the current menu or ask the bar team about the actual ABV.

Wheat beer: gentle malt and yeast character

“Wheat beer” is an umbrella for different traditions. German wheat beers may suggest banana and clove, while Belgian witbier may show citrus peel or spice. Body ranges from crisp to full; it is not all “banana milk.” Ask whether today’s beer is closer to banana and clove or citrus and spice.

Fruit beer: fruit can join the show

Some beers use fruit or fruit ingredients; others naturally show fruit-like aromas. A fruit beer can be sweet, tart or crisp and still taste clearly like beer. Ask whether it is sour or sweet, how prominent the fruit is, and what the ABV is.

IPA: let the hop aroma speak

IPA often puts hops in the spotlight, with citrus, tropical fruit, flowers, herbs or resin. Bitterness varies, and a vivid aroma does not always mean intense bitterness. Hazy IPA can still be bitter. Ask for hop aroma with a softer bitter finish if that is what you prefer.

Takeaway

These are three routes, not a ranking. Say whether you want hop aroma, wheat and yeast notes, or fruit character.

Specific flavour and alcohol details vary by beer, so use the brewer’s description and today’s menu.
