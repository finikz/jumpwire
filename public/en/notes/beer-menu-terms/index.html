Why Does a Craft Beer Menu Feel Like a Foreign-Language Test?

No exam required. Start with a few useful questions.

JUMPWIRESeptember 14, 20263 min read

A menu full of abbreviations can look like a vocabulary test. It is not. Start with two things: style names are broad families, and measurements are clues—not a complete description of what you will taste.

Style names are family names, not flavour promises

IPA is known for hop character, but aroma and bitterness vary widely. Wheat beer covers many recipes; ask whether the yeast brings banana and clove or whether the beer leans toward citrus and spice. Stout and porter often use dark malts, but body and roast intensity differ. Saison can be dry, fruity or spicy. Gose is associated with wheat, acidity and salt, but the balance varies.

A few numbers worth knowing

ABV means alcohol by volume. IBU is an analytical measure related to bittering compounds, not a precise forecast of how bitter a beer will taste to you. Body describes the beer’s weight and texture. None of these numbers replaces the beer’s own description or your palate.

Questions you can use at the bar

“Is the aroma more citrus, floral or tropical?” “How noticeable is the bitterness?” “Is the body light or full?” “What is the ABV?” You can also say what you usually enjoy and what you would rather avoid.

Takeaway

A beer menu is not a test. A useful question is already enough to find a starting point.

Style references and brewery descriptions are useful guides, but individual beers can vary within a style.
