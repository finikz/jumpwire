Industrial Beer vs. Craft Beer: What Are People Looking For?

They are not two sides in a battle between good and evil.

JUMPWIRESeptember 14, 20263 min read

Large-scale production can offer reliable supply and affordable products. Independent or smaller breweries may find it easier to try different recipes, styles and seasonal releases. Scale alone does not determine quality; ingredients, process, hygiene and the beer itself still matter.

“Craft” can mean different things in different markets

The term craft beer does not have exactly the same meaning in every country or industry. Instead of treating a label as a quality guarantee, look at the beer description and information from the brewer.

People may be looking for more than one thing

Some people want a familiar, consistent, crisp lager. Others are curious about fruit, acidity or roasted malt. Some care about events, a place to meet or time with friends. Choices can change because of flavour, culture, social settings and variety; it is not simply that “young people like one kind of beer.”

A better question: what do I want tonight?

Be specific: crisp or rich? Familiar or new? Are alcohol strength, bitterness or serving size important to you? Whether you choose an industrial beer, a craft beer or a non-alcoholic drink, you can find a better answer by starting with what you want.

Takeaway

Craft beer does not tell you what you have to drink. It gives you more ways to answer “What do I feel like?”
