Beer for Hot Pot: More Than a Case of Cold Bottles

Start with the broth, then think about your own spice tolerance.

JUMPWIRESeptember 14, 20263 min read

The challenge at a hot-pot table is not finding beer; it is that broth, dipping sauces and spice tolerance vary from person to person. Clear broth, tomato and mala are different situations. One “universal pairing” should not make the whole table suffer.

Read the broth: heat, fat, savouriness and acidity

Pronounced bitterness or higher alcohol may make the heat of mala broth feel stronger. With a rich, oily broth, look for carbonation and a refreshing finish. Tomato or pickled-cabbage broth is already tart, so consider whether a beer’s acidity works with it rather than simply adding more acid.

If some friends prefer mild spice and others want chilli oil, start with the person who is least comfortable with heat. Pairing should make dinner more enjoyable, not turn your palate into an extreme-sports event.

Three useful directions—not a rigid chart

For spicy broth, begin with something crisp, lower in bitterness and lighter in alcohol. For rich broth, see whether bubbles, acidity and the finish feel refreshing. With a delicate or clear broth, choose a beer that leaves the food some room. These are ideas, not guarantees: recipes, chillies, sauces and personal taste all change the result.

Ask before the pot starts boiling

Tell the bar team: “We are having mala/clear broth and want something less bitter/more refreshing. Could you also tell me the ABV?” Check the day’s beer descriptions before choosing. For a group, make sure non-drinkers have an alcohol-free option. Do not drive after drinking.

Takeaway

Hot pot is not a beer jury. Say what is in the pot, say what you like, and let everyone choose for themselves.
