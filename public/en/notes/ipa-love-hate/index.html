Why Do People Love and Hate IPA?

A hop-forward family can still taste many different ways.

JUMPWIRESeptember 14, 20263 min read

IPA (India Pale Ale) is often known for a more pronounced hop character. Hops can contribute bitterness and aromas such as citrus, flowers, pine, herbs or tropical fruit. Variety, timing, fermentation and maturation all affect the result.

Two common routes, two different personalities

American IPA often has clear hop aromas—citrus, flowers, pine or fruit—and bitterness may be more noticeable. Breweries balance aroma, bitterness and malt in different ways.

Hazy IPA often has a hazy appearance, a fuller impression and prominent fruit aromas. Its bitterness may feel softer than some traditional IPAs, but it is still hop-forward and not necessarily bitterness-free.

Why IBU and your palate may not agree

IBU is an analytical measure related to bittering compounds. It is useful as a reference, not a precise prediction of how bitter a beer will taste to you. Body, malt sweetness, aroma, temperature and personal sensitivity all affect perception. One person may call the same number “just right,” while another finds it intense.

Ask for the IPA you actually want

If you are cautious about bitterness, ask for a hop aroma that feels softer and check the ABV. If you enjoy clear bitterness, ask which IPA has the most pronounced bitter finish and whether its body is dry or full. If you are undecided, ask whether it is a traditional hop-led profile or a fruitier Hazy IPA.

Takeaway

“IPA” points to a family of beers. The individual recipe is what you will taste.

Use the brewery’s description and style references as guides; bitterness and aroma vary across individual IPAs.
