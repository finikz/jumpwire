Off-Flavours in Beer: Character or a Problem?

Learn the names, but do not rush to a verdict.

JUMPWIRESeptember 14, 20263 min read

Beer can surprise you, but not every unexpected flavour is “character.” Some sensory notes can point to a process issue. Recognizing a word is not the same as diagnosing a product, and this article cannot assess the safety of a particular beer.

A few common sensory clues

Light-struck beer is often described as skunky and can be associated with exposure to light. Oxidation may suggest stale cardboard or lost freshness. Diacetyl can resemble butter or butterscotch, though styles differ in whether it is expected. DMS is often described as cooked corn. These are common descriptions, not one-word diagnoses.

A more reliable tasting sequence

Check the style and product description first, then consider storage and packaging. Record aroma, flavour and finish in neutral terms instead of immediately calling a beer “bad” or “premium.” Comparing a fresh example or asking a trained professional gives you a better basis for judgement.

If the package or product seems abnormal

Do not continue tasting a beer with damaged packaging, a broken seal, leaks or an obviously unusual condition. Ask the seller or producer. Do not explain every problem away as “wild fermentation.” This article cannot determine whether a particular beer is safe; seek professional medical advice if you feel unwell.

Takeaway

Flavour can be unexpected. Product condition is not something to guess your way through. Check the facts before drawing a conclusion.
