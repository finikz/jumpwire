CRAFT BEER NOTES · 06

Yeast: Beer’s Best Scene-Stealer

Hops may be centre stage; yeast can rewrite the plot.

JUMPWIRESeptember 14, 20263 min read

Yeast uses fermentable sugars in wort to produce ethanol and carbon dioxide. It also creates other compounds, some of which bring fruity or other flavour notes. Strain, temperature and brewing management all affect the result.

Ale and lager: a common distinction, not a ranking

Ale yeast is traditionally associated with warmer fermentation conditions; lager yeast is commonly fermented cooler and followed by cold maturation. Yeast and temperature shape flavour, but both families are broad. Ale does not automatically mean strong, and lager is not one single taste.

There are also spontaneous and mixed-fermentation methods. They do not mean the yeast was forgotten; brewers deliberately use different microbes and time to create more complex results.

Taste the yeast without guessing the strain

Describe what you actually smell and taste: fruit, spice, dryness, foam and finish. Then check the beer description for its fermentation method or style. Describing your experience is more reliable than guessing the yeast strain—and this is not a microbiology exam.

Takeaway

Hops wave from the stage while yeast rewrites the plot backstage. Both matter.
